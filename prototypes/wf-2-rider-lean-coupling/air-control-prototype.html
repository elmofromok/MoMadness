PROTOTYPEDoes Rider lean rotate the Bike?

Throwaway prototype for issue #2. When the Bike is airborne, does moving the Rider apply reaction torque to the Bike, or are the two rotations independent? This builds model 3: one stick input both throws the Rider's weight, which produces reaction torque scaled by Lean coupling, and applies torque straight to the Bike, scaled by Direct Bike authority. Coupling 0 with authority turned up is model 2, independent. Coupling 1 with authority 0 is model 1, fully coupled, angular momentum conserved. Ride it, sweep the two sliders, and find where the air-save wants to sit. The answer to record is the model, and if coupled, roughly what coupling value.

Start here

  1. Click the picture once, so the keyboard reaches it.
  2. The Bike rides and jumps by itself. You never steer it. The only thing you control is the Rider, who is the orange figure.
  3. While it is in the air, hold W to throw the Rider forward, or S to throw them back. That single question is what this whole page exists to answer: does moving the Rider rotate the Bike underneath them?
  4. Then it lands. If the Bike's angle is within 25° of the ground it lands clean. Otherwise it crashes and respawns a moment later. The bar at the top left of the picture shows how wrong the landing will be if you land right now, and the white prompt tells you which key you want.
  5. Then work down the guided runs below, in order. Each one sets the sliders for you and drops you into the same jump, so the only thing that changes between runs is the physics.

Deterministic fixed step at 120 Hz, in the spirit of ADR-0002: no frame-rate-coupled forces. Pitch and roll are two independent one-degree-of-freedom axes, which is a simplification. Real 3D flight adds gyroscopic cross-talk this does not simulate, so a coupling value found here is a starting point for the Unity tune rather than a final number. What should transfer intact is the choice of model.
