# Racing is cut from the base game

MoMadness takes its cue from Motocross Madness (1998), which was a racing game:
five event types, over thirty tracks, and a career mode built on winning them.
The base game ships none of that. There is no racing, no events, no career, no
collectibles, and no score. What ships is an authored sandbox: open themed zones
seeded with things worth riding at and things worth noticing, where discovery is
the only reward.

The appeal being chased is MM1's open Baja terrain and its separate bike and
rider physics, not its track list. Supercross and National were tight linear
stadium tracks, which is the opposite of the open rolling terrain this project
is for, and they are also the most expensive content a solo developer could
choose to author.

## Considered options

- **Rebuild MM1's content list.** Clearest target, and roughly thirty tracks of
  authoring for one person. It also invites a comparison against nostalgia that
  a remake cannot win.
- **Keep the physics, design everything else fresh.** Rejected only in that the
  open-terrain mode was worth keeping deliberately rather than by accident.
- **Keep Baja's open terrain and the physics, drop the rest.** Chosen.

## Consequences

Removing racing removes the only completion signal the source game had. Nothing
is left to gate progression on, so everything unlocks from the start, and any
future unlock system has to bring its own earning mechanism with it.

Content density becomes the entire production cost. An authored sandbox with
thin content is an empty world, and there is no scoreboard to distract from it.
This makes "what does a finished zone look like" a real design problem rather
than a formality.

A reader who knows the source game will expect tracks and will not find them.
That is deliberate.
